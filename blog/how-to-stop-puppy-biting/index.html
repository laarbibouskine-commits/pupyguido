How to Stop Puppy Biting: The Pause, Redirect, Reward Method

Training October 2, 2026 · 5 min read

Sharp little teeth on your hands and ankles can make a new puppy feel like a tiny shark. The good news: mouthing is a normal part of how puppies explore and a normal part of teething. It is also very trainable.

Why do puppies bite?

  • Exploring. Puppies use their mouths to learn about the world.
  • Teething. Chewing can feel good on sore gums.
  • Play. Puppies play with each other using their mouths.
  • Overtired or overexcited. Biting often spikes late in the day.

The method: Pause, Redirect, Reward

Step 1: Pause

The moment teeth touch skin, stay calm and stop the game. Go still, or stand up and step away. No shouting, no hitting, no pinning the puppy down.

Step 2: Redirect

Offer an appropriate chew toy or tug toy. This shows your puppy what they can bite.

Step 3: Reward

When your puppy chews the toy, praise warmly and give a treat when appropriate. Puppies repeat what pays off.

Make it easier

  • Keep several chew toys within reach in every room.
  • Plan naps. A well-rested puppy bites less.
  • Keep play sessions short and end them before your puppy gets wild.
  • Give frozen or safe chew options for teething, chosen to suit your puppy's size and age.

Common mistakes that make biting worse

  1. Rough play that winds the puppy up.
  2. Waving hands or feet in front of the puppy's face.
  3. Punishing, which can create fear and mistrust.
  4. Inconsistency: allowing biting sometimes and not other times.
  5. Skipping rest and mental exercise.

When to ask a professional

If biting is intense, comes with growling or stiff body language, or you are worried about safety, ask a qualified trainer or your veterinarian for help.
